Isn't virtually everything about ontology -> dynamics -> epistemology?

---

Yes—if by that you mean:

**What is** → **How it changes / behaves** → **What can be known about it**

That triad shows up almost everywhere.

* **Ontology** = what exists; the state space; the entities and constraints.

  * What are the “things”?
  * What degrees of freedom exist?
  * What distinctions matter?

* **Dynamics** = rules of transformation; how the system evolves.

  * What interacts with what?
  * What are the update rules?
  * What generates trajectories?

* **Epistemology** = what an observer can infer from interaction with the system.

  * What is observable?
  * What is identifiable?
  * What remains hidden / underdetermined?

That’s basically:

$x_{t+1}=f(x_t)$

with:

* ontology = the space (x) lives in,
* dynamics = the function (f),
* epistemology = the inverse problem: infer (x) or (f) from observations.

Or in Bayesian language:

$$
\text{prior structure} \rightarrow \text{generative process} \rightarrow \text{posterior belief}
$$

Or in physics:

$$
\text{configuration space} \rightarrow \text{equations of motion} \rightarrow \text{measurement}
$$

Or in your **Ukubona tensor** language:

* **Scalar / Action** → ontological commitment (“something is there”)
* **Vector / Intention** → directionality / dynamic force
* **Matrix / Attention** → selective observation
* **Tensor / Sensation** → structured evidence
* **Eigenmode / Reaction** → stabilized knowledge / belief

Even philosophy often secretly reduces to this:

* Parmenides obsessed over ontology (“what is”).
* Heraclitus obsessed over dynamics (“everything flows”).
* Immanuel Kant obsessed over epistemology (“what can be known”).

And the modern synthesis is: you can’t separate them.

Your **ontology constrains your dynamics**:

* if you think only particles exist, you write particle equations;
* if you think fields exist, you write field equations.

Your **dynamics constrains your epistemology**:

* chaotic systems make long-term prediction impossible;
* reversible systems permit retrodiction;
* noisy systems limit inference.

And your **epistemology feeds back into ontology**:

* what you can measure often determines what you claim exists (think Wave Function vs Hidden Variables).

So yes—but I’d sharpen it:

**Almost every intellectual project is really**
$$
\textbf{Ontology} ;\leftrightarrow; \textbf{Dynamics} ;\leftrightarrow; \textbf{Epistemology}
$$
as a **closed loop**, not a chain.

That’s why control theory, neuroscience, politics, theology, and even personal identity all rhyme:
“What am I?” → “How do I change?” → “How do I know?”

That may be the universal compression.
